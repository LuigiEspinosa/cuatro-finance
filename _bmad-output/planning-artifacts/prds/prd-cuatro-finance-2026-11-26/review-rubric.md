# PRD Quality Review: Cuatro Finance

Reviewer: rubric walker, against the bmad-prd validation checklist. Inputs: `prd.md`, `addendum.md`, and `.memlog.md`. Stakes as recorded: one owner, chain-top for UX, architecture, and epics, sized by its FRs and concerns.

## Overall verdict

This is a decision-ready, substantive PRD: every ruling traces to a recorded owner decision and names what it gave up, the NFRs carry numbers instead of adjectives, and the non-goals and deferrals do real work. The risk sits where UX, architecture, and story creation will lean hardest: Claude's write boundary is stated twice and the two statements disagree (FR-54 against FR-30, FR-31, and FR-42), and the 90-day forecast that every cash buffer check reads (FR-44) lacks the rules that would make two builds agree. Closing those two, plus the medium precision gaps in deduplication, undo, budget months, and client authorization, would make it ready to build from.

## Decision-readiness: strong

Decisions are stated as decisions and sourced. Each feature cites the ruling that shaped it ("decision 8", "decision 4"), and the addendum's decision record (§2) names the rejected alternative for each decision. Trade-offs carry what was given up: the §5.11 description says the assessment recommended a read-only connection and that write access ships with guardrails instead; §5.7 replaces the solver with deterministic rules (S8-15); §9.2 gives every deferral a reason and a revisit trigger, and a separate "Cut, not deferred" line closes the old plan's dead ends.

The three NOTE FOR PM callouts sit at real tensions (the write tools against SM-C1, capture on the go, the release order), not at safe checkpoints. The six Open Questions are genuinely open, and each names who resolves it and where in the chain. Someone pushing back on write access, on the absence of an in-app LLM, or on figure-free alert emails finds the objection acknowledged rather than dodged.

## Substance over theater: strong

No persona theater: one user, and one fictional protagonist whose journeys each drive FRs and an end-to-end check (NFR-10). The Vision could not swap into another PRD: it names Colombian accounts, the split between Claude's reasoning and the money layer's arithmetic, and the absence of bank sync as the reason capture runs through the owner. The NFRs are thresholds, not adjectives: a 30 minute idle and 24 hour absolute session (NFR-4), a 7 day adoption delay (NFR-6), a 2 day staleness label (NFR-8), p95 latency at 20,000 transactions and Lighthouse floors (NFR-9), and coverage by module (NFR-10); FR-15 adds RPO and RTO. The Colombian money rules (§4) are product rules the FRs cite, not decoration.

The furniture is light: §7.3 repeats NFR-12 in one line, and the sixth job in §2.1 restates "Why it matters". Neither costs anything.

## Strategic coherence: adequate

The thesis is explicit and the features follow it: Claude is the interface and the app does the arithmetic (§1, A-1), so the PRD invests in one money layer, one staging step shared by every capture channel, and bounded, logged, undoable writes. The fix-first sequencing follows from a core that never worked end to end (addendum §2, decision 1), and the counter-metrics are unusually honest: SM-C2 and SM-C3 refuse engagement and breadth as goals.

Three things weaken the arc. The MVP is one block whose test clock has no defined start, and the suggested first release does not stand alone. The primary metric cannot be computed from what the app records. And the metric set stops at capture, so the advice half of the main feature goes unmeasured.

### Findings
- **medium** The thesis test has no start date, and the suggested first release does not stand alone (SM-1, SM-2, §9.1, §10 NOTE FOR PM, SM-C3): SM-1 and SM-2 run "for three consecutive months after launch", and "launch" appears nowhere else. §9.1 makes all twelve features one MVP, while SM-C3 says "a reconciled ledger beats breadth" and the release order that would test the ledger first is only a suggestion. That first release also leans on second-release FRs: import sends near duplicates and unmatched transfer candidates to the review queue (FR-28, FR-26), which is FR-32 in §5.5; conversational capture categorizes by rules (FR-56), which are FR-31 in §5.5; the root page lands on the dashboard (FR-13), which is FR-45 in §5.9. *Fix:* define launch, for example as the first deploy of the first release, so the SM-1 clock starts with the working ledger; move FR-31, FR-32, and a minimal FR-45 into the suggested first release, or state that the MVP ships as one release.
- **medium** SM-1 cannot be computed from what the app records (SM-1, FR-29): "within 7 days of the statement arriving" needs an arrival date, but FR-29 records only a statement's closing balance and date. "No unexplained difference" has no counterpart in FR-29, where a difference is either zero or a list of unmatched transactions, with no way to mark one explained. "Every account" includes types that may never receive a statement, such as a crypto account. SM-1 is the primary metric and the trigger for the demo's return (§9.2), so it has to be computable. *Fix:* measure from the statement closing date plus a fixed allowance, or record when a statement was imported; drop "unexplained" or add an FR-29 consequence for annotating a difference; scope SM-1 to accounts that receive statements.
- **low** The metrics stop at capture (§11; addendum §2, decision 1): SM-1 and SM-2 test the interface thesis well. The advice half of the main feature (debt strategy, budget split, savings plans) has only SM-3, a floor, and SM-4, which counts that "a budget exists", an activity measure. The PRD also never says what follows an SM-1 or SM-2 miss, although the decision record's reason for decision 1 is that the owner never used the previous version. *Fix:* replace SM-4 with an outcome the ledger already holds (for example, months in which the planned monthly extra was paid, or the share of budgets that closed within their limit), and add one line naming the response to an SM-1 or SM-2 miss.

## Done-ness clarity: adequate

Most FRs meet the bar, and many exceed it: consequences are concrete and checkable, often with numbers or named cases (FR-9's page bounds, FR-15's RPO and RTO, FR-20's pending TRM, FR-28's row counting, FR-39's line reasons, FR-42's detection thresholds, FR-48's figure-free emails). The baseline FRs state behavior without adjectives, and no "gracefully", "reasonable", or "user-friendly" phrasing survives.

This is still the weakest of the seven dimensions, because its gaps cluster where story creation will start: the planning computations (the forecast, budget months, and several rules that are named but never defined) and the edges of the AI connection (deduplication across channels, undo of compound writes, and what authorizes a tool call).

### Findings
- **high** The forecast lacks the rules that would make two builds agree (FR-44; read by FR-39, FR-40, FR-45, FR-53, UJ-4, UJ-8): FR-44 names inputs and confidence levels but not (a) how a monthly variable spending estimate is spread over the days of a month, (b) which account it lands on, given that card spending raises a card's debt and reaches a savings account only at the card payment, (c) the monthly extra, which is missing from the input list although UJ-4 says "the payment plan and forecast update" once Claude saves it, and (d) how a day that mixes high, medium, and low inputs gets the confidence level that "each day shows". §3 says the forecast "marks any day below" the cash buffer; FR-44 says "the first day". Every buffer check in the PRD reads these numbers (the plan's breach flag, each scenario's lowest balance, the dashboard's low point, both journey edge cases), and NFR-10's fixtures "computed outside the code" cannot be written without them. *Fix:* add FR-44 consequences for daily spreading (for example, even per day, or on the days a category usually posts), account allocation, the payment plan (minimums and monthly extra) as an input, a day confidence rule (for example, the lowest confidence among the inputs that move that day), and one answer to "first day" versus "any day".
- **medium** The cross-channel deduplication rules overlap, and one channel is unchecked (§3 Duplicate, Near duplicate, Match; FR-28; FR-11; §5.4): a staged row with the same account, date, and amount as a transaction Claude recorded, under a different description (the usual case, since a statement's description rarely matches the words the owner used), is both a near duplicate (§3) and a match (FR-28). UJ-5 counts the two separately, so a precedence exists, but nowhere in writing. Separately, §5.4 promises "the same transaction never posts twice across Claude, manual entry, and import", yet FR-11 gives manual entry no duplicate check, so a transaction told to Claude and then entered by hand posts twice, and only a later reconciliation difference (FR-29) would reveal it. *Fix:* state that a match takes precedence over a near duplicate when the existing transaction's source is Claude or manual; give FR-11 the likely-duplicate check of FR-56, or narrow the §5.4 sentence.
- **medium** Undo is promised for every write but defined for none of the compound ones (FR-55; FR-27, FR-28, FR-30; §7.2): FR-55 says "the owner can undo any logged write" and gives one dependency example. The PRD's writes include compound ones: confirming an import posts many rows at once, confirming a match keeps one transaction and records the statement's description and date with it, and a category merge moves "every transaction, rule, and budget". Whether undo works per call or per row, what undoing a merge restores, and whether an undo can itself be undone are all open, and §7.2's safety claim (no write tool makes a change that cannot be undone) is only as strong as those answers. *Fix:* add FR-55 consequences: one undo per write call that restores every record the call changed; a merge records the records it moved so undo can split them back; an undo is itself a logged write.
- **medium** Sessions and connected clients are not separated in the requirements (FR-2, NFR-4, FR-51, NFR-5): FR-2 says "every tool rejects a request whose session has not completed TOTP", and NFR-4 ends a session "after 30 minutes idle and after 24 hours in any case". Read literally, every tool call depends on a session that ends within a day, which contradicts NFR-5's 30 day unused disconnect and UJ-3's evening capture. Addendum §3.8 says "Sessions follow NFR-4; connected clients follow NFR-5", but the PRD never states what a tool call must carry or how long a client's authorization lives. *Fix:* rewrite FR-2's tool clause as a connected-client rule (a tool call carries an authorization granted through a password and TOTP sign-in, holding the needed scope and not revoked), and say in NFR-4 or NFR-5 which limits govern that authorization.
- **medium** Budget months are underdefined (FR-33, FR-34, UJ-7, SM-4): the PRD does not say whether a limit carries into the next month or is written per month (UJ-7 has Claude write "next month's budgets", and SM-4's "a budget exists for every month" is trivially met if limits carry forward); when a month closes for FR-34, and whether a closed month's actual changes when a statement imported days later adds rows dated inside it, which SM-1's 7 day window makes routine; or whether a purchase in several cuotas counts against its purchase month in full or one cuota per month. Each choice changes the data model and the history Claude reads in UJ-7. *Fix:* add FR-33 and FR-34 consequences for carry-forward, the close rule with late rows, and cuota purchases.
- **medium** Rules that are named but never defined (FR-11, FR-21, FR-25, FR-31, FR-35, FR-42, §3 Duplicate): each leaves the story writer to invent a rule that fixtures depend on. FR-11 rejects amount text "ambiguous under that format" without saying what is ambiguous in es-CO. FR-25 says editing either side of a transfer "keeps the pair consistent", which is unclear for a COP to USD transfer whose sides keep their own currencies (FR-26). The duplicate's "description key" is never defined. FR-31 orders rules "the narrowest rule first" without saying whether an account, an amount range, or longer text is narrower. FR-35's sinking fund "starts its next cycle" with no cycle length (§3 says only "a goal that repeats"). FR-21 shows a "cost basis" with no method for a partial sell. FR-42's "near 7, 14, or 28 to 31 days" and "normalized merchant" carry no tolerance or normalization rule. *Fix:* one sentence or a short example table per item; for FR-11, a handful of accepted and rejected strings.
- **low** NFR-9 does not say what "responds" measures (NFR-9): "any screen responds within 1 second and any tool call within 2 seconds at the 95th percentile" leaves open whether the percentile covers screens, and whether a screen's second ends at the server response, the first render, or the largest paint. *Fix:* name the measured event and apply the percentile to both clauses.
- **low** The end-to-end check per journey has no stand-in for Claude (NFR-10; addendum §3.7; UJ-2, UJ-3, UJ-4, UJ-7, UJ-8): five of the eight journeys run through a Claude app (claude.ai, Claude Desktop, or Claude Code), which a check run on every push cannot operate. *Fix:* say that those checks drive the AI connection with a scripted client making the journey's tool calls, and that FR-50's three-client acceptance is a recorded manual check.

## Scope honesty: strong

Omissions are explicit. §8 names what the product will never do (move money, sync banks, run an in-app LLM, act as an investment advice engine), §9.2 gives each deferral a reason and a revisit trigger, and its "Cut, not deferred" line keeps the old plan's dead ends from returning by drift. Conditional scope is labeled where it applies: FR-23 hangs on Open Question 1, and §9.1 repeats the condition. The investment boundary is honest: the Vision promises "a savings and investment plan", and §8 answers that "Claude reasons, the app computes".

All ten assumptions carry inline IDs and an index, and the owner confirmed them. Open items (six questions, three notes, no unconfirmed assumption) are low for a build-ready, chain-top PRD, and each names who resolves it. One gap: part of decision 8 is in scope only by implication.

### Findings
- **low** The fix-first scope omits part of decision 8 (§9.1, §5.1; addendum §4, §5): the decision log puts upgrade steps U1 to U6 in the fix-first scope, but §9.1 lists only "FR-1 to FR-17 and FR-63", and only U2 appears in an FR (FR-8); NFR-6 implies the rest. Deferred items 3, 4, 5, and 8 sit in addendum §5 with no MVP or post-MVP call. *Fix:* add U1 to U6 to the §9.1 fix-first line, and mark each remaining deferred item as MVP or as the epics step's call.

## Downstream usability: adequate

The glossary is the PRD's strongest downstream asset: 73 terms in five groups, used as defined in nearly every FR, under a stated rule that no section introduces a synonym. IDs are contiguous and unique, every FR carries exactly one brownfield tag, each journey lists the FRs it exercises, each SM lists the FRs it validates, and positional IDs resolve through addendum §1. Sections stand alone through glossary terms; there is no "see above".

Two problems would leak into the next steps: the write boundary that architecture will build its tool list from disagrees with the FRs, and some journey steps, which NFR-10 turns into checks, have no FR behind them. Smaller drift is listed under Mechanical notes.

### Findings
- **high** The write boundary disagrees with the FRs that grant Claude writes (FR-54; FR-30, FR-31, FR-42; §7.2; addendum §3.8): FR-54 enumerates Claude's writes, including "categorize and create rules" and "subscription statuses", and both §7.2 ("Claude's writes are bounded (FR-54)") and addendum §3.8 rest the safety argument on that list. But FR-30 lets "the owner, or Claude with write scope" add, rename, merge, and archive categories; FR-31 makes rules editable "through the write tools"; FR-42 says "the owner or Claude adds yearly subscriptions". None of these is in FR-54. An architect who builds the tool list from FR-54 drops three capabilities; one who builds from the feature FRs widens the boundary, category merge included, which is the largest compound write in the PRD. *Fix:* make FR-54 the single list: either add category management, rule editing, and adding a subscription to it (with an undo rule for merge, see the FR-55 finding), or remove the Claude grant from FR-30, FR-31, and FR-42.
- **medium** Journey steps with no FR behind them (UJ-1, UJ-8; FR-35, FR-36, FR-43, FR-44, FR-53): UJ-1 enters "the cuota purchases still running on her last statement", but FR-36 covers only a purchase recorded with its number of cuotas: nothing covers cuotas already paid, or how a running purchase relates to the card's opening balance, which risks counting that debt twice. UJ-1 lists FR-43, yet no journey sets the cash buffer that UJ-4 and UJ-8 depend on. UJ-8's edge case has "the goal plan" flag one month and Claude propose a split, but FR-35 offers only on-track status, FR-44 marks only the first day below the buffer, FR-53 returns only "a goal's needed monthly contribution", and "goal plan" is not in §3. UJ-8's comparison of holding COP with buying USD each month has no compute tool, so under A-1 and §7.2 ("The figures Claude cites come from compute tools") it must quote no figures, which the journey does not say. *Fix:* add a running-purchase consequence to FR-36 (cuotas remaining and outstanding principal, counted once against the opening balance); add the per-month buffer check to FR-35 or FR-53 and define "goal plan" in §3, or reword UJ-8; add a cash buffer step to UJ-1; say in UJ-8 that the currency comparison is qualitative.

## Shape fit: strong

The shape matches the product. A single-operator tool would normally make journeys overhead, but here the interface is a conversation with Claude, so the journeys carry behavior a capability list would miss (the account question in UJ-3, the staged statement in UJ-4, undo across an import match in UJ-6), and NFR-10 turns each into a check. The brownfield shape is handled by a tag on every FR (all 63 carry exactly one), by the rule that baseline items state only required behavior, and by leaving the patch or replace choice for defective screens to the epics step. The chain-top role is served by addendum.md, which keeps technical choices and UX notes out of the PRD body while keeping them traceable.

This review did not check the tags or the assessment pointers against the assessment itself, which was outside its inputs. For a brownfield PRD the rubric needs those references to be accurate, so another reviewer should confirm them.
