# PRD Quality Review: Cuatro Finance

Post-final update, 2026-11-27. Scope: the working-tree changes to `prd.md` and `addendum.md` that apply the post-final rulings (`.memlog.md` lines 94 to 117), read against the rest of both files. Findings already listed in sections 3 to 6 of `review-post-final-2026-11-27.md` are not repeated unless the applied fix is wrong, incomplete, or creates a new problem. Line numbers point at the working copy.

## Overall verdict

The rulings are in place, and the PRD stays decision-ready: every owner call is recorded with the alternative it rejected (addendum §2.4), and most new rules carry numbers a test can hold. The risk sits at the seams between new and existing rules, where four highs would change what gets built: the duplicate test still compares an amount the PRD never freezes, an accepted or mitigated advisory has no stated effect on the deploy gate, the forecast can count a card's share of the monthly extra twice, and the one job list can neither meet the 26-hour backup bound nor catch a stalled job runner. Only the advisory gap needs a new owner call; the other highs and the eleven mediums, mostly undefined states at the same seams, each have a fix that follows from the text.

## Decision-readiness: strong

The rulings read as decisions, not considerations. Addendum §2.4 (lines 61 to 75) lists the ten owner calls, each with what it rejected and why (PF-6: "Rejected: later months at their minimums, which pays more now but leans on lower-confidence estimates"), and its lows entry records what was rejected and what was deferred to which step. L-22 turned the open items triage from a coach assumption into an owner acceptance (memlog line 110), so the seven Open Questions and the two NOTE FOR PM callouts now have owners on record. The one thin spot: for two calls, §2.4 names what the rejected option would have cost but not what the chosen one gives up.

### Findings

- **low** Chosen options' costs missing for PF-8 and PF-11 (addendum §2.4, lines 67 and 68): PF-8's chosen rule lowers the debt by the whole payment until the split is entered, so meanwhile debt reads low and net worth high by that payment's interest and charges. PF-11's latest TRM makes a cross-currency goal's progress move with the rate, so a goal can stop showing as reached with no new transaction. The rejected options' costs are stated; these are not. *Fix:* add one clause to each entry naming what the chosen option gives up.

## Substance over theater: strong

Nothing this run added is furniture. NFR-13 is specific to this product: unattended operating system security updates, a monthly rebuild from a patched base, and an away rule tied to NFR-5 and FR-29 (line 1032). The write cap comes with numbers and an alert (FR-54, line 915), FR-42's definitions are numeric (line 771), and NFR-3 now names all six A and AA criteria new in WCAG 2.2, each with the behavior it checks (line 1015). The single fictional protagonist still drives every journey.

## Done-ness clarity: adequate

Most new consequences are testable as written: FR-63's drill cadence (line 414), FR-24's status filter (line 504), FR-42's interval bands (line 771), NFR-9's 15.000-transaction trigger (line 1021), and the two named fixtures in NFR-10 (line 1026). The gaps sit where a new rule meets an existing one, and there a story writer would have to invent the answer. Four of them would change what gets built.

### Findings

- **high** The duplicate test compares an amount the PRD never freezes (§5.4 FR-28, lines 559, 562, 564; §3 Duplicate and Cleared, lines 155 and 159): a cleared transaction keeps its row's date and description key "unchanged by later edits", but both duplicate tests still match "the same account and amount as a cleared transaction", which is the transaction's own amount. FR-28 never says which amount a confirmed near-amount match keeps (a Claude capture within 15 percent, A-11). Line 564 says "it keeps its own date for budgets and its locked rate" and keeps the row's date, description, and description key beside it, but not the row's amount. Read literally, the capture's amount stays: the statement's reconciliation shows a difference, and re-importing the overlapping file stages that row as new. That breaks line 559's promise that re-importing "skips every row already cleared". An amount edit after clearing does the same. *Fix:* in FR-28, confirming a match sets the transaction's amount to the row's (SM-C1 already excludes match confirmations), and the cleared transaction keeps the row's amount beside its date and description key for both duplicate tests (§3 Cleared, Duplicate, Near duplicate).
- **high** Accepting or mitigating an advisory has no stated effect on the deploy gate (§6 NFR-6, line 1018; FR-8, line 323; NFR-13, line 1032): NFR-6 keeps "No deploy goes out with a known advisory" and adds that while one is open "the owner accepts it, mitigates it, or takes the affected part of the deployment (the AI connection or the whole app) offline". It never says whether an advisory the owner accepted or mitigated, recorded with its ID, still blocks deploys. Read literally it does, so from then on every deploy that removes no known advisory is held until a fix version exists. That includes any code fix and NFR-13's monthly rebuild, which NFR-13 exempts from the 7-day delay but not from this gate. The deploy gate behind FR-8 cannot be built without choosing. *Fix:* an owner ruling, stated in NFR-6. Either an accepted or mitigated advisory, recorded with its ID, stops blocking deploys until a fix version exists, or it keeps blocking them and NFR-13's rebuilds are exempt.
- **high** The forecast can count a card's share of the monthly extra twice, and a loan's share has no landing day (§5.8 FR-44, lines 789 and 793; FR-39, line 731): line 789 lists "the monthly extra (the payment plan is an input, with the full monthly extra in each later month, FR-39)" as an input, and line 793 puts "plus the card's share of the monthly extra (FR-39)" inside each later card payment. Nothing says the second is how the first reaches cash. An implementation that applies both lowers the paying account twice for the card's share, and FR-39 then holds back too much of this month's extra. For a loan or the mortgage, nothing says on which day a later month's share lands. The required fixture, "a forecast across several card cycles" (NFR-10, line 1026), would encode whichever reading its author picks. *Fix:* in FR-44, state that the monthly extra reaches cash only as each debt's share under the payoff method, counted once: inside a card's projected payment, and beside a loan's installment on its payment due date.
- **high** The one job list cannot meet the 26-hour backup bound, and nothing catches a stalled job runner (addendum §3.2, lines 104, 106, 109; NFR-11, line 1030; NFR-13, line 1032; FR-15, line 385): the list sets "Backup and restore check age checks: daily", so FR-15's "no backup within 26 hours" can surface up to a day late. These backstops also run in the job runner they watch. NFR-11 fails the health endpoint only on "a failed self-check", and a job with no success "shows in the app", which tells no one. If the runner stops while the owner is away, a time NFR-13 says runs "unattended", backups stop and no alert goes out. *Fix:* run the age checks hourly, beside the self-check. In NFR-11, make the health endpoint fail when any job in the one list has no success within twice its interval, so the external monitor alerts on a stalled runner. Give alert sends (line 109), which have no interval, their own bound or leave them out of that rule.
- **medium** The widened confirmation set leaves the dates of its balances and its creation-time state undefined (FR-65, lines 964 and 965; FR-18, line 438; FR-29, lines 577 and 580): the set names "statement entry (closing balance, and for a card its minimum payment and printed payment due date)" but not the closing date, and an opening balance but not the opening date. Yet each balance holds on its date: FR-29 starts the next period from the closing date, and FR-48 anchors the Release 1 payment due alert on the opening date (line 847). A Claude-entered date therefore takes effect at once while the balance it dates waits. For a new account, "rejecting it leaves the previous value, if any, in force" has no previous value, and FR-18 allows a later change only as "an adjustment with a reason". So nothing defines what the account shows while its opening balance or starting debt waits, or how a rejected one is entered again. *Fix:* confirm each balance with its date as one entry, like "a statement entry's values together". State what an account shows while its opening balance or starting debt waits, and that a rejected one is entered again as a creation value, not as an adjustment.
- **medium** Three void and undo paths for cleared transactions are undefined (FR-25, line 517; FR-28, line 559; FR-55, lines 930 and 931). First, once the owner "keeps the row skipped", the voided transaction takes "no part in these tests", so the next overlapping import stages the same row as new. Second, "offers to match the two" applies Match to two posted transactions, but §3 defines a match only between a staged row and a transaction (line 156). Third, a range undo "applies only after the owner confirms" once, yet each cleared transaction it voids needs FR-25's choice between posting the row as new and keeping it skipped. *Fix:* keep a row the owner kept skipped as a skipped row that the duplicate tests still compare against, so L-10's rule for voided records stands. Define the offer: the restored transaction takes over the later one's clearing row and the later one is voided, or both stay. Have a range undo leave each such choice in the review queue, as a void through Claude does.
- **medium** The write cap does not say what one call is or how the windows run (FR-54, line 915; A-13, line 1201): "at most 100 write calls an hour and 500 a day" does not say whether staging a statement's rows is one call or one call per row. It also does not say whether the hour and the day are rolling or clock windows, though "the first refusal in each hour or day" suggests clock windows. If each row is a call, Claude staging one long statement, or the missed statements of NFR-13's return step, would hit the hourly cap and raise the alert. *Fix:* state that one staging call carries one statement's rows and counts once, and that the windows are clock hours and Bogotá days.
- **medium** NFR-1 says "once" and then rounds twice (§6 NFR-1, line 1013): "A computed value is rounded half-even once: when it is stored, or when it is shown if it is never stored" is followed by "Stored COP values keep their centavos, and showing one rounds it half-even to the peso". A stored computed COP value is therefore rounded to the centavo and then to the peso, which can land one peso away from a single rounding of the exact value. The fixtures computed outside the code need to know which result is right. *Fix:* say that a computed COP value rounds to the centavo when stored and its display rounds the stored value, or store computed COP values in whole pesos.
- **medium** No rule covers a goal in another currency fed by a two-account planned contribution (FR-35, lines 664, 669, 670, 672; FR-41, lines 758 and 762): when the source and funding accounts differ in currency, a planned contribution's single "amount" has no stated currency, and A-11's "within 10 percent of the occurrence's amount" does not say which side of the transfer it compares. FR-35 does not limit the funding account to a savings account, but the conversion rule covers only the TRM, so a crypto funding account has no basis. With the latest TRM, "shows as reached" can come and go as the rate moves. *Fix:* state that a planned contribution's amount is in its source account's currency and is compared on the source side, limit funding accounts to savings accounts, and say whether reached stays once shown.
- **medium** The payment plan has no rule for when later months' assumed extras breach the buffer on their own (FR-39, line 731; NFR-10, line 1026): the plan "schedules the largest part that keeps its paying account at or above its cash buffer on each of the next 90 days". When the full monthly extra assumed in a later month breaches the buffer by itself, no part of this month's extra qualifies. "Naming the first day it would breach the buffer" would then name a breach that this month's extra does not cause. The NFR-10 fixture covers only the partial case ("a later month's extra decides how much of this month's is held back"). *Fix:* when no part qualifies, the plan holds back all of this month's extra and says the breach comes from the committed monthly extra in a later month, naming that day, so the owner can lower it. Add that case to the fixture.
- **medium** R-5's repricing rule does not say whether it touches purchases at their own rate (§4 R-5, line 223; FR-36, lines 688 and 697): FR-36 stores a purchase's own EA rate "when it differs from the card's", a promotional rate for example, and R-5 decides per card "whether a change in its rate reprices pending cuotas" without excluding those purchases. The Release 2 recompute (line 697) and its fixtures depend on the answer. *Fix:* in R-5, repricing applies only to purchases at the card's rate, and a purchase with its own rate keeps it.
- **medium** The contents of "what the forecast leaves out" are not defined (FR-44, lines 790 and 791; FR-35, line 671; FR-40, line 743; FR-45, line 813; FR-53, line 906): five FRs now return this list beside every lowest balance, but only unestimated categories are named as members. Other exclusions in the PRD are candidates: a generated transfer side that "stays pending, outside balances" (FR-26, line 530), an archived account, which "leaves pickers, the forecast, and the payment plan" (FR-12, line 362), a Claude-entered input still awaiting confirmation (FR-65), and a loan payment awaiting its split (FR-37). *Fix:* list the members in FR-44, at least unestimated categories and inputs awaiting the owner's confirmation.
- **medium** Undoing a creation can void a record that others depend on (FR-55, line 929): "Undoing a write that created a record voids that record", and the refusal rule only covers "a later write changed one of those fields". Undoing Claude's creation of an account with transactions, a category with transactions or rules, or a goal with contributions would void the parent and leave its dependents counting. Nothing defines what a voided account, category, or goal does to the records under it (FR-12 covers only archive and delete), and "counts in SM-C1" applies only to transactions. *Fix:* refuse to undo a creation while live records depend on it, naming them as the later-write rule does, and limit the SM-C1 clause to transactions.
- **low** The reconciliation's write record does not say which writes or which date (FR-29, lines 578 and 581): "each write that changes a transaction in its statement period" may or may not include creating or voiding one, which is how most differences close. It also does not say whether a cleared transaction falls in the period by its own date or by its row's date, which line 578 now uses for the cleared balance. *Fix:* "each write that creates, changes, or voids a transaction placed in the period by the date the cleared balance uses".
- **low** The Release 2 alert setting may cover the failed backup alert (FR-48, line 848): the edited sentence lists what the setting "never turns off" (new connected client, repeated failed attempts, write limit) and leaves out failed backup or restore check and payment due. A reader can therefore make the backup alert switchable. *Fix:* say that the setting covers only the budget and statement expected types, or add the backup alert to the never-off list.
- **low** A price change can conflict with FR-41's tolerance and account (FR-42, line 775; FR-41, line 762): a charge 5 to 10 percent off already fulfils its occurrence under A-11's 10 percent tolerance. Also, "lets that charge fulfil its occurrence" applies to any charge with the subscription's description key, including one on another account, which FR-41 never lets fulfil an occurrence ("a posted transaction on the event's account"). *Fix:* limit the price change test to the subscription's account, and say that a charge already fulfilling its occurrence keeps doing so whichever way the item is resolved.
- **low** "Adds to the cost basis" reads like the option the owner rejected (FR-21, line 472; addendum §3.6, line 144): read as a total, adding the fee's COP value to the cost basis needs a price at the fee's moment, which addendum §2.4 rejected for PF-12. The clause "the total COP cost stays" corrects this, but only for a careful reader. *Fix:* "a fee paid in the asset lowers the quantity and leaves the total COP cost unchanged, so the average cost per unit rises".
- **low** NFR-13's base advisory has no named source, and its return step misses disconnected clients (NFR-13, line 1032; addendum §3.9, line 171): NFR-6 now names the source of a known advisory, but "an advisory at high or above in that base" names none, the same gap PF-15 closed for packages. Also, by the owner's return NFR-5 will have disconnected any client unused for 30 days, not only the ones whose grant lapsed. *Fix:* have the architecture step name the base advisory source in §3.9, and write "reconnects any connected client whose grant lapsed or that NFR-5 disconnected".
